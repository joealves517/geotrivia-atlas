In the heart of East China, some 40 kilometers northwest of the bustling metropolis of Hangzhou, lies Deqing. Here, amidst the industrial hum of a textile colony, an extraordinary cultural phenomenon has taken root: the Xiquets de Hangzhou. Their name, a blend of Catalan and Chinese, translates evocatively to "Club of human towers of the peach flower village" (桃花庄 - 人塔俱乐部). This group of performers, defying both gravity and geographical distance, has mastered the intricate, centuries-old Catalan art of *castells*, or human towers, transforming a Spanish tradition into a vibrant Chinese spectacle. It is a story of vision, cross-cultural collaboration, and the sheer human will to build something spectacular, one person atop another.

The genesis of this improbable fusion traces back to 2009, when Qian Anhua, the astute president of the Chinese textile company Antex (De Qing) Fashion Clothes Co. Ltd, embarked on a journey to Catalonia. There, he encountered the breathtaking tradition of *castells* – towering human structures built by communities in a display of strength, balance, and courage. Anhua was captivated. Recognizing its potential as a unique social activity to foster camaraderie and spirit among his employees, he resolved to transplant this daring art form to his industrial hub in Deqing. The seed was planted, and by May 2010, the Xiquets de Hangzhou began their ascent, catalyzed by a visit from the esteemed Colla Vella dels Xiquets de Valls to Shanghai for the International Exhibition.

The art of *castells* demands absolute trust and synchronized effort, where each participant, from the sturdy base to the agile child at the very apex, plays a crucial role. For the employees of Antex, this became more than just a pastime; it evolved into a demanding discipline requiring physical prowess, mental focus, and an unwavering commitment to the collective. The transformation from textile workers to *castellers* was profound, as they embraced the intricate techniques and the spirit of mutual reliance that are the hallmarks of this Catalan heritage. What began as an entrepreneurial initiative blossomed into a deeply ingrained cultural practice within the community, demonstrating how shared endeavor can transcend origins.

The Xiquets de Hangzhou quickly rose to prominence, not just within China, but on the global *castells* stage. They are now recognized as the most successful *colla exterior* – a human tower team operating outside the traditional Catalan-speaking regions. Their achievements speak volumes of their dedication and skill. They have successfully built formidable structures such as the 3 de 9 amb folre, a nine-level tower with a supporting base; the 2 de 8 amb folre, an eight-level tower with two people per level and a supporting base; the solid 3 de 8; the complex 5 de 7; and the elegant 3 de 7 amb l'agulla. These accomplishments place them among the elite practitioners of this demanding art.

Crucial to their success has been the enduring and close relationship forged with their Catalan mentors, Colla Vella dels Xiquets de Valls. This bond is far from a one-way street; it represents a vibrant exchange of knowledge and support. Colla Vella made significant journeys to Deqing in both 2010 and 2015, offering direct guidance and sharing the nuances of their craft. In return, the Xiquets de Hangzhou traveled to Catalonia in 2012, notably assisting Colla Vella in the prestigious contest of *castells* of Tarragona, a display of their growing skill and the depth of their solidarity. Another reciprocal visit occurred in 2015, further cementing this unique cross-continental partnership.

Witnessing the Xiquets de Hangzhou perform is to observe a powerful spectacle where tradition meets innovation. The sight of dozens of individuals, clad in their distinctive uniforms, meticulously assembling a multi-tiered human structure against a Chinese backdrop, is a potent symbol of global cultural dialogue. It highlights how artistic forms can migrate, adapt, and flourish in unexpected environments, creating new meanings and shared experiences. Their performances are not merely acrobatic feats; they are expressions of community, discipline, and the universal human aspiration to reach for the sky, literally and figuratively.

![Xiquets de Hanghzou, Concurs 16](/images/atlas/xiquets-de-hangzhou-catalan-human-towers-in-china-inline-2.webp)

From a visionary textile entrepreneur's journey to a distant land, to the collective effort of hundreds building living monuments, the story of the Xiquets de Hangzhou is a compelling narrative of cultural transplantation and triumph. It illustrates how a specific regional art can find a new home and achieve international acclaim, proving that the human spirit of collaboration knows no geographical bounds. In Deqing, the echoes of Catalan culture resonate in each carefully placed hand and foot, creating a lasting, vibrant bridge between two distinct worlds.
