Along the northern coast of Taiwan, just outside Tamsui in New Taipei's Sanzhi District, rows of brightly painted fiberglass pods once stood perched on concrete stilts facing the sea. Shaped like stacked flying saucers with rounded porthole windows and sweeping external staircases, the cluster looked like a film set built for a retro space colony. Yet no vacationer ever unpacked a suitcase inside. Before a single guest could check in, construction halted, leaving behind what urban explorers and internet forums eventually dubbed the ruins of the future.

Ground broke on the Sanzhi UFO Houses in 1978, backed by the Hung Kuo Group. Developers envisioned an avant-garde seaside retreat modeled loosely on Finnish architect Matti Suuronen's Futuro houses, examples of which still survive elsewhere in Taiwan, notably in nearby Wanli District. The target clientele was specific: U.S. military officers stationed across East Asia looking for a modern coastal escape near Taipei. Each modular pod was designed to offer futuristic comfort, elevated above the humid shoreline on reinforced concrete pillars.

![三芝飛碟屋](/images/atlas/sanzhi-ufo-houses-inline-1.webp)
_Figure 1: Modular fiberglass pods of the Sanzhi UFO Houses overlooking the northern Taiwan coast in 2008, shortly before demolition._

By 1980, the dream collapsed. Severe financial losses stalled work on the site, and a string of fatal car crashes and worker suicides during construction cast a grim shadow over the unfinished resort. Local residents soon attributed the tragedies to a fateful act of roadwork: builders had reportedly sliced a traditional Chinese dragon sculpture in half near the resort entrance to widen the access road, a violation of local geomancy. Other rumors held that the coastal parcel sat atop an old burial ground for Dutch colonial soldiers.

Whatever the true mix of economics and engineering trouble, the Hung Kuo Group walked away, leaving the saucer-shaped shells to salt spray, typhoons, and creeping vegetation. Over the next twenty-eight years, the empty pods became an accidental landmark. Crews filmed scenes for the 1987 action movie White Phantom among the concrete stilts, MTV shot music footage on location, and photographers traveled to Sanzhi to document the peeling pastels and shattered windows.

![三芝飛碟屋01](/images/atlas/sanzhi-ufo-houses-inline-2.webp)
_Figure 2: A row of weathered saucer-shaped structures along the Sanzhi coastline, illustrating the modular pod architecture inspired by 1960s Futuro designs._

In late 2008, local authorities moved to clear the aging structures to make way for a new commercial seaside resort and water park. Despite an online petition pleading with officials to preserve at least one pod as a museum piece, bulldozers began tearing down the complex on December 29, 2008. By 2010, the last saucer had been razed to the ground.

Though the physical pods have vanished from the Sanzhi shoreline, their silhouette endures in digital memory. Beyond archival photographs, the site's distinctive coastal layout inspired a multiplayer map called Pod in the 2012 video game Call of Duty: Black Ops II, preserving Taiwan's strangest abandoned resort long after the concrete stilts were swept away.
