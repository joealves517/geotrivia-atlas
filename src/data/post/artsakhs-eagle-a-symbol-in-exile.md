In the complex geopolitical landscape of the South Caucasus, national symbols often carry weight far beyond mere heraldry. The coat of arms of the Republic of Artsakh is one such emblem, a visual narrative woven from history, landscape, and identity, now facing a profound test of its own. Adopted in 1992, this symbol has witnessed the tumultuous journey of a self-declared republic, whose very existence has been challenged, dissolved, and then defiantly reasserted from exile.

At the heart of Artsakh's emblem is a powerful eagle, its wings spread, crowned with an ornate circlet that speaks of sovereignty and historical lineage. Positioned centrally on the eagle’s chest is a shield, a protective device that here cradles the very essence of the land. Within this shield, a panorama of rugged mountains unfolds, evoking the dramatic terrain that defines the region. Beneath this landscape, the vertical stripes of the Republic of Artsakh's flag are depicted, a direct assertion of its national identity within its own symbolic framework.

Dominating the shield, above the mountain range and flag, are the distinctive stone heads of "Granny and Gramps" (Tatik yev Papik). These iconic figures, drawn from the "We Are Our Mountains" monument in Stepanakert, the capital, are not merely statues but cultural anchors, representing the enduring spirit and ancestral ties of the people to their land. They are a silent, stony declaration of presence and heritage, overlooking the symbolic landscape of Artsakh.

![Մեծ Քիրս](/images/atlas/artsakhs-eagle-a-symbol-in-exile-inline-1.webp)

The eagle’s talons firmly grasp a bounty of the land: sheaves of wheat and clusters of grapes. These agricultural products are not just decorative elements; they signify prosperity, sustenance, and the deep connection of the people to the fertile earth of Artsakh. Encircling the entire composition is a golden ribbon, bearing an inscription in Armenian: "Լեռնային Ղարաբաղի Հանրապետություն-Արցախ, Lerrnayin Gharabaghi Hanrapetut’yun-Arts’akh," which translates to "Artsakh Republic of Mountainous Karabakh." This inscription explicitly names the entity and its geographical context, leaving no ambiguity about its identity.

This meticulously designed coat of arms was officially adopted on November 17, 1992, through a decision by the Supreme Council of the Republic. Its usage and representation were further formalized and regulated by the "Charter on the State Coat of Arms of the NKR," providing a legal framework for its role as a national symbol. For decades, it represented the aspirations and official presence of the republic.

![Army Artsakh](/images/atlas/artsakhs-eagle-a-symbol-in-exile-inline-2.webp)

The narrative of Artsakh and its emblem took a dramatic turn following an Azerbaijani offensive on September 19, 2023. This led to an agreement for Artsakh to dissolve itself by January 1, 2024. However, the story did not end there. In a powerful assertion of continued identity, the government of Artsakh established a government-in-exile in Yerevan in October 2023. Critically, on December 22, 2023, this government-in-exile repealed the very decree that would have dissolved Artsakh's institutions, signaling a refusal to fade into history.

Thus, the coat of arms of Artsakh, with its proud eagle, ancient stone figures, and rich agricultural symbols, persists as more than a historical artifact. It remains a living emblem for a government operating beyond its traditional borders, a visual declaration of an enduring identity that, despite immense challenges, refuses to be erased. Its continued existence reflects a complex interplay of politics, heritage, and the unyielding spirit of a people.
